SECTION 1 — BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION
================================================================================

1.1  THE GLOBAL DIABETES CRISIS
-------------------------------
Diabetes mellitus is one of the most pressing chronic health crises of the
21st century. According to the International Diabetes Federation (IDF) Atlas
(2021), approximately 537 million adults globally were living with diabetes in
2020, and this number is projected to reach 783 million by 2045. The annual
global economic cost exceeds USD 1 trillion in direct and indirect healthcare
expenditure. In India specifically, over 77 million adults are affected, making
it the second-largest diabetic population worldwide.

Type 2 diabetes, which accounts for over 90% of cases, is substantially
preventable through early detection and lifestyle intervention. However,
diagnostic processes remain largely reactive — dependent on expensive laboratory
tests, specialist appointments, and clinician interpretation — placing enormous
burden on overburdened healthcare systems.

1.2  THE PROMISE AND PITFALL OF AI IN HEALTHCARE
-------------------------------------------------
Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) offer transformative
potential for early diabetes risk stratification. Classifiers trained on
routine clinical features (e.g., BMI, glucose, blood pressure, age) can
predict diabetes onset with accuracies exceeding 85–90%. However, the adoption
of these tools in clinical practice has been severely limited by a fundamental
problem: the "black box" nature of most ML models.

Clinicians cannot audit WHY a model predicted high diabetes risk for a specific
patient. Patients cannot understand the factors driving their personalised risk
assessment. Regulatory bodies (FDA, EU AI Act, NICE) cannot formally validate
opaque stochastic systems for use as Software as a Medical Device (SaMD). This
gap between ML predictive capability and clinical usability is referred to as
the "Interpretability Gap."

1.3  EXISTING SOLUTIONS AND THEIR LIMITATIONS
----------------------------------------------
The most prominent attempt to bridge this gap is TalkToModel (Slack et al.,
2023), a conversational XAI system that enables non-technical users to query
ML models through natural language dialogue. TalkToModel achieved 82% clinician
preference for dialogue over dashboards — validating the conversational
interaction paradigm.

However, TalkToModel fundamentally relies on Large Language Models (LLMs):
the T5 transformer for semantic parsing and the BART language model for
response generation. This LLM dependency introduces four critical limitations:

  (1) HALLUCINATION RISK: LLMs are generative models — they produce fluent,
      plausible text that may be factually incorrect. In clinical settings,
      hallucinated medical claims (e.g., fabricated drug interactions, false
      dosage recommendations) are directly dangerous to patient safety.

  (2) POOR CALIBRATION: TalkToModel achieves a Brier Score of 0.189 —
      significantly worse than MedTalk's 0.142. In risk stratification,
      miscalibrated probabilities lead to systematic under- or over-treatment.

  (3) REGULATORY MISALIGNMENT: FDA 510(k) submissions and EU AI Act Article 6
      both require deterministic, auditable, formally validatable algorithms.
      LLM outputs are stochastic (different each run) and cannot be formally
      tested to specification.

  (4) DOMAIN RIGIDITY: TalkToModel's dialogue policy is learned from data and
      cannot encode hard regulatory constraints such as anti-redlining laws
      (finance), sentencing guidelines (judiciary), or anti-discrimination
      underwriting rules (insurance). Domain-specific safety requirements must
      be guaranteed, not learned.


SECTION 2 — WHAT IS MEDTALK?
================================================================================

MedTalk is a domain-agnostic, four-layer, deterministic conversational AI
system that enables transparent, auditable, and interactive risk assessment
across regulated sectors. It was designed, built, and validated by the
undergraduate team as a B.Tech Major Project at KIET Group of Institutions.

MedTalk's central thesis is: "Responsible AI is an engineering discipline,
not a domain-specific afterthought." The same architectural principles that
guarantee safe, interpretable dialogue in healthcare can be instantiated — with
only configuration changes — in finance, judiciary, and insurance.

2.1  CORE PRINCIPLES
---------------------
  • DETERMINISM: Every prediction from the same input produces the same output.
    No stochastic LLM components. Fully reproducible for regulatory submission.

  • TRANSPARENCY: The Random Forest prediction is explained via mathematically
    grounded SHAP (Shapley values) and LIME local approximations, not opaque
    neural generation.

  • AUDITABILITY: Every user interaction (feature inputs → SHAP attributions →
    EDAQ question selection → refined prediction) is logged in a complete
    audit trail for clinical governance and regulatory review.

  • CROSS-SECTOR PORTABILITY: Domain adaptation requires only (1) a question
    template dictionary and (2) constraint rules — not model retraining.

2.2  THE EDAQ INNOVATION
-------------------------
The Explanation-Driven Adaptive Questioning (EDAQ) algorithm is MedTalk's
primary intellectual contribution. EDAQ addresses a fundamental limitation of
static XAI dashboards: they present all features equally without guiding the
clinician toward the most clinically relevant, actionable, and uncertain
information for a specific patient.

EDAQ dynamically generates the next most relevant follow-up question at each
dialogue turn through a six-step process:

  Step 1 — Feature Uncertainty Quantification:
            Computes per-feature uncertainty scores using missing data rates
            and ensemble prediction variance.

  Step 2 — Impact-Threshold Filtering:
            Features with |SHAP value| < 0.05 are excluded (noise floor).

  Step 3 — Domain Constraint Filtering:
            Protected attributes (race, gender, zip code as proxy) and non-
            modifiable features are excluded per sector-specific rules.

  Step 4 — Cross-Domain Semantic Mapping:
            Feature names are mapped to sector-appropriate question templates
            (e.g., "glucose" → "What was your last fasting blood glucose?" in
            healthcare vs. "income" → "What is your household income?" in
            finance).

  Step 5 — Multi-Objective Prioritisation:
            Candidates are ranked by: (SHAP impact × uncertainty) / response
            burden, ensuring the highest-value questions are asked first.

  Step 6 — Compliance Check:
            A final fairness filter ensures no discriminatory question enters
            the dialogue stream before presentation.

EDAQ runs in under 100 milliseconds on standard CPU hardware — compared to
TalkToModel's GPU-dependent 1,200ms inference time. This makes MedTalk
deployable in resource-constrained settings: rural clinics, developing-nation
judiciaries, and community credit unions.


SECTION 3 — SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

MedTalk is structured as four sequential, domain-agnostic processing layers:

LAYER 1 — PREDICTION LAYER
  Technology: Scikit-learn Random Forest (500 trees, max_depth=18)
  Function  : Produces a calibrated probability of the target outcome
              (e.g., P(diabetes=1) for a given patient feature vector)
  Components:
    • Data Preprocessing Pipeline (zero-value imputation, StandardScaler)
    • SMOTE class balancing for skewed datasets
    • RandomizedSearchCV hyperparameter optimisation (200 iterations)
    • Platt Scaling post-hoc calibration

LAYER 2 — ATTRIBUTION LAYER
  Technology: SHAP (TreeExplainer) + LIME (LimeTabularExplainer)
  Function  : Computes local feature importance for each individual prediction
  Components:
    • TreeSHAP for efficient, exact Shapley value computation on Random Forests
    • LIME for model-agnostic local surrogate approximations
    • Fidelity-Based Selection: selects SHAP or LIME based on which produces
      higher local fidelity for a given sample

LAYER 3 — ORCHESTRATION LAYER (EDAQ)
  Technology: Pure Python rule-based algorithm
  Function  : Transforms attributions into prioritised, domain-compliant,
              actionable follow-up questions; manages multi-turn dialogue;
              enforces fairness guardrails; maintains audit log
  Components:
    • EDAQ core algorithm (Algorithm 2)
    • Explanation-Driven Risk Assessment orchestrator (Algorithm 1)
    • Interactive Refinement with Fairness Guardrails (Algorithm 3)
    • Audit Logger

LAYER 4 — INTERFACE LAYER
  Technology: Streamlit (Python web framework)
  Function  : Patient-facing and clinician-facing web interface
  Components:
    • Feature input form (demographic + clinical fields)
    • Risk visualisation (gauge, probability bar, risk category badge)
    • SHAP waterfall plot (inline attribution visualisation)
    • EDAQ question cards (interactive dialogue turns)
    • Audit trail viewer (downloadable compliance log)
    • Domain selector (healthcare / finance / judiciary / insurance)


SECTION 4 — DATASETS
================================================================================

4.1  PRIMARY DATASET: PIMA INDIANS DIABETES DATASET (PIDD)
------------------------------------------------------------
  Source    : UCI Machine Learning Repository / NIDDK (National Institute of
              Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases)
  Records   : 768 female patients of Pima Indian heritage, aged ≥21
  Features  : 8 clinical variables
               • Pregnancies — Number of times pregnant
               • Glucose — Plasma glucose concentration (2-hour OGTT)
               • BloodPressure — Diastolic blood pressure (mm Hg)
               • SkinThickness — Triceps skinfold thickness (mm)
               • Insulin — 2-hour serum insulin (μU/ml)
               • BMI — Body Mass Index (weight in kg / height in m²)
               • DiabetesPedigreeFunction — Genetic risk score function
               • Age — Patient age (years)
  Target    : Outcome (0 = non-diabetic, 1 = diabetic)
  Class Split: 65.1% non-diabetic (500) / 34.9% diabetic (268)
  Note      : Zero values in Glucose, BloodPressure, SkinThickness, Insulin,
              and BMI are physiologically implausible and are treated as missing.

4.2  SECONDARY DATASETS (SYNTHETIC — CROSS-DOMAIN VALIDATION)
--------------------------------------------------------------
  Finance   : N=500 records; structure based on UCI German Credit Dataset;
              features: age, income, employment_duration, credit_history,
              loan_amount, loan_duration, housing_type
  Judiciary : N=400 records; structure based on ProPublica COMPAS study;
              features: age, charge_degree, prior_offenses, supervision_level
  Insurance : N=350 records; typical underwriting features: age, BMI,
              smoking_status, bmi, num_claims_prior, vehicle_age

SECTION 6 — TECHNICAL SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

6.1  SCOPE
  • Primary clinical application: Type 2 diabetes risk stratification
  • Validated clinical user population: Doctors, nurses, and medical trainees
  • Primary deployment environment: Web browser (Streamlit); CPU-compatible
  • Cross-domain configurations: Healthcare, Finance, Judiciary, Insurance

6.2  LIMITATIONS
  • Single external dataset for healthcare (PIDD); broader clinical validation
    on diverse ethnic populations is required before production deployment
  • Synthetic cross-domain datasets for finance and judiciary; real-world
    institutional data partnerships are needed for full validation
  • Static feature vectors only; temporal disease progression is not modelled
  • User study population is from a single institution; multi-site studies
    are planned as future work

6.3  FUTURE SCOPE
  • Federated learning integration for multi-institution training without
    sharing raw patient data
  • Temporal modelling with LSTM + SHAP for time-series EHR data
  • Mobile interface (Progressive Web App) for point-of-care deployment
  • FDA 510(k) formal submission pathway
  • Partnership with financial/judicial institutions for real-world validation
